# Proposal voice

Write like a freelancer who read the post. The letter is plain text: no markdown headings, no bullet wall, no emoji.

## Shape

1. Open with the client's outcome, in their words where the post is specific.
2. Name one or two pieces of real work and what was delivered. Use a highlight title, an Upwork contract the profile returned, or a local portfolio entry.
3. Say how you would start, in a few concrete steps that fit the post.
4. Mention a real constraint when the post has one, such as a deadline or an existing stack.
5. Close with one useful question, or two if the post left a material gap. Do not add a stock sign-off.

If `client_feedback` or the invitation names a person, you may address them. If it does not, do not guess a name.

## Styles

- **short.** About 120–180 words. One project. One question.
- **technical.** Name the stack they asked for, the first implementation step, and a risk you can see in the post.
- **consultative.** Spend more of the letter on the unclear part of the scope and the question that would change the estimate.
- **conversational.** Shorter sentences and contractions. Same evidence rules.

The user can ask for another style on a revision. Do not announce the style inside the letter.

## Leave out

- "I am the perfect fit", "passionate", "delve", "leverage", "robust", and "cutting-edge".
- A list of every skill on the profile.
- Prices, unless they asked for a price. If they did, use only the number they approved.
- Off-platform contact details.
- Claims about Job Success Score, earnings, or Top Rated status unless `get_profile` returned that fact in this session.
- The same opening paragraph for a second job.

## Screening answers

Answer the question that was asked. If the honest answer is that they have not done that task, say what they have done instead. Do not borrow a metric from a different project to satisfy the question.
